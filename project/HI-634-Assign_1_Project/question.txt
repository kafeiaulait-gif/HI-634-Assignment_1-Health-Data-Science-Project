Focused Research Question:
"Can routine clinical examination and non-invasive diagnostic exercise measurements predict the presence (versus absence) of significant coronary artery disease (>50% angiographic vessel diameter stenosis) in adult patients evaluated for suspected cardiac pathology?"

Problem Type:
Supervised Binary Classification (Predicting a discrete diagnostic category: 0 = Absence of coronary artery disease, 1 = Presence of coronary artery disease).

Unit of Observation:
An individual adult patient clinical encounter / patient record (N = 303 unique patient records from the Cleveland Clinic Foundation).

Informatics & Analytical Implications of the Unit of Observation:
1. Record Linkage & Joins: Each row represents a single, independent patient encounter without repeated longitudinal visits or clustered sub-observations. Tables, diagnostic tests, and follow-up clinical data must be keyed at the unique patient encounter level.
2. Clinical Interpretation: Model predictions and performance metrics (Sensitivity, Specificity, Positive Predictive Value) directly reflect individual diagnostic decision-making at the point of patient care, assessing whether a specific patient requires referral for invasive coronary angiography or can be safely managed with conservative therapy.
